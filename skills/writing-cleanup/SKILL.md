Make the text read as if a knowledgeable person wrote it for this reader. Change how things are said, never what is said.

## Rules that never bend

- Keep every fact, number, name, date, link, quote and claim exactly as it is.
- Keep the author's voice: person (I, we, you), spelling, tone, formatting, length limits.
- Never change quotations, titles, legal text or code.
- Never narrate the pass. Hand back the cleaned text, not a report on it, unless asked.
- If a sentence carried only padding, delete it. If it carried a fact, keep the fact.

## What to look for

The patterns below are symptoms. Fix what the sentence is doing, not just the word that tripped you.

Content
- Puffed-up significance: a plain fact followed by why it "matters", "reflects a broader trend" or "marks a turning point". Cut it, or replace it with a specific fact.
- Vague authority: "experts say", "many believe", "studies show" with no source. Name the source or cut the claim.
- Promotional tone in a factual text: "stunning", "world-class", "seamless", "cutting-edge". Say what it does.
- Answering an objection nobody raised: "It's not about X, it's about Y." State Y.
- Lists of three that exist for rhythm, not meaning. Keep the items that carry information.
- A closing paragraph that restates the piece. Cut it, or end on the next step.

Words and phrasing
- Stock verbs for "is": "serves as", "stands as", "functions as", "boasts". Use "is" or "has".
- Inflated words: "delve", "crucial", "pivotal", "landscape", "leverage", "robust", "tapestry", "testament", "underscore", "foster", "realm". Use the plain word or the specific one.
- Filler openers: "It's important to note that", "In today's fast-paced world", "When it comes to". Start with the point.
- Hedging stacked on hedging: "may potentially", "could possibly". Pick one, or state it.
- Synonym cycling: calling the same thing three different names to avoid repetition. Use one name.

Punctuation and format
- Dash-heavy sentences. Rewrite with commas, colons, parentheses or full stops. Don't swap in another kind of dash.
- Title Case Headings. Use sentence case.
- Bullet lists where each bullet starts with a bold label and a colon. Write the point as a sentence, or use a table.
- Bold scattered through running text. Keep bold for the one thing a skimmer must see, if any.
- Emoji as bullet points in professional text.

Chat leftovers
- "Certainly!", "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like", "As an AI". Remove them.
- Placeholder brackets or instructions left in the text. Fill them or remove them.

## What not to "fix"

Good grammar, a formal register, one transition word, a single em dash in a long piece, or a sentence that simply states a fact are not signs. Leave clean sentences alone. Over-correcting makes text worse.

## Workflow

1. Read the whole piece once for what it's trying to do and who reads it.
2. Go sentence by sentence through the patterns above.
3. Read it again start to finish, out loud in your head, for rhythm and for anything that now sounds wrong.
4. Check that every fact, number, link and quote is still there and unchanged.
